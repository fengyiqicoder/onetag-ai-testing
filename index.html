The guide

General guidance for healthy pet dogs, not veterinary advice. Anything urgent: call a vet.

Feeding

How often should I feed a puppy?

Puppies under 6 months eat 3 meals a day, 6 to 12 months 2 meals, adults 1 or 2 meals at the same times each day. Measure portions by the bag's guide for the dog's expected adult weight, then adjust to body condition: you should feel the ribs easily but not see them.

Which foods are dangerous for dogs?

Chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol (in sugar-free gum and some peanut butters), macadamia nuts, alcohol, cooked bones and raw bread dough. If a dog eats any of these, call a vet or an animal poison line right away, even if the dog seems fine.

Can I give my dog table scraps?

Small amounts of plain cooked meat, carrot, apple slices without seeds, green beans and plain rice are fine as treats. Keep all treats under 10% of daily calories. Skip anything fatty, salty or spiced, and anything on the dangerous-foods list.

When should I switch from puppy food to adult food?

Small breeds at about 9 to 12 months, large breeds at 12 to 18 months. Change over 7 to 10 days, mixing a little more of the new food in each day, so the stomach has time to adjust.

How much water does a dog need?

Roughly 50 to 60 ml per kilogram of body weight a day, more in heat or after exercise. Keep fresh water available at all times. A sudden, lasting increase in drinking is a reason to see a vet.

Health

What vaccines does a puppy need and when?

The core shots (distemper, parvovirus and adenovirus, usually combined as DHPP) at about 8, 12 and 16 weeks, then a booster at one year. Rabies between 12 and 16 weeks where the law requires it. Your vet sets the exact schedule for your area.

When can my puppy go outside?

Short outings in your arms can start right away, because meeting people, sounds and places early matters. Walks on the ground can begin 1 to 2 weeks after the last puppy shot, around 16 to 18 weeks. Avoid dog parks and unknown dogs until then.

How do I know if my dog is sick?

Normal body temperature is 38 to 39.2°C. See a vet the same day for not eating for more than a day, repeated vomiting, diarrhea with blood, unusual tiredness, pale gums or trouble breathing.

How often should my dog see the vet?

Puppies every 3 to 4 weeks until the shots are done. Adults once a year. Seniors, from about 7 years for large breeds and 9 for small, twice a year with blood work.

Do I need flea, tick and worm prevention?

Yes, year-round in most climates. A monthly chewable or spot-on covers fleas and ticks. Deworm on the vet's schedule: puppies every 2 weeks until 12 weeks, then monthly until 6 months. Where there are mosquitoes, monthly heartworm prevention too.

When should I spay or neuter?

Commonly between 6 and 12 months. Many vets now wait until 12 to 18 months for large breeds so the joints finish growing. Decide the timing with your vet.

Training

How do I house-train a puppy?

Take the puppy out after waking, after eating, after play and every 1 to 2 hours at first. Reward within 2 seconds of finishing outside. Never punish accidents; clean them with an enzyme cleaner so the smell doesn't invite a repeat. Most puppies are reliable by 4 to 6 months.

Why does my puppy bite my hands, and how do I stop it?

Mouthing is normal play and teething. When teeth touch skin, say "ouch" calmly, go still and offer a toy instead. If it continues, end play for 20 to 30 seconds. With consistency it fades, usually by 6 to 7 months.

How long should training sessions be?

3 to 5 minutes for puppies, up to 10 or 15 for adults, a few times a day. End on a success. Use pea-sized treats so the dog doesn't fill up.

Should I crate-train?

A crate the dog can stand up and turn around in, introduced with treats and meals inside, becomes a safe spot and makes house-training easier. Never use it as punishment. Puppies under 6 months should not be crated more than 3 to 4 hours at a stretch during the day.

My dog pulls on the leash. What do I do?

Stop walking the moment the leash goes tight, wait for slack, then carry on. Reward the dog for walking next to you. A front-clip harness helps while you train. Pulling takes weeks of consistent practice to change.

How do I teach "come"?

Start indoors with no distractions. Say the dog's name and "come" once in a happy voice, reward generously on arrival, and never call the dog for something unpleasant. Practise on a long line before going off-leash anywhere.

Daily life

How much exercise does my dog need?

Most adult dogs need 30 to 60 minutes a day; working and sporting breeds up to 2 hours. Puppies: about 5 minutes of structured walking per month of age, twice a day, plus free play. Sniffing walks and puzzle toys tire a dog as much as running.

How often should I bathe and groom my dog?

Bathe every 4 to 8 weeks unless the dog is dirty; more often dries the skin. Brush short coats weekly and long or double coats several times a week. Trim nails every 3 to 4 weeks; clicking on the floor means they are too long. Brush teeth a few times a week.

How do I help my dog with separation anxiety?

Practise short absences and lengthen them gradually. Leave without a fuss and come back calmly. Leave a stuffed food toy and exercise the dog before you go. If the dog panics, destroys things, drools or howls for long stretches, ask a vet or a certified trainer; some dogs need medication alongside training.

How do I introduce a new dog to my cat or other dog?

Separate spaces first and swap bedding for a few days so they learn each other's scent. Then short leashed meetings on neutral ground, rewarding calm behaviour, ending before anyone gets tense. Full freedom together can take weeks.

Can my dog be outside in hot or cold weather?

Above 30°C, walk early morning and evening, test the pavement with the back of your hand for 7 seconds, and never leave a dog in a car. Below 0°C, keep walks short for small, thin-coated or old dogs; a coat helps. Shivering, lifting paws or slowing down means go inside.

Emergencies

What are the signs of bloat?

A swollen, hard belly, retching without bringing anything up, restlessness and drooling, most often in deep-chested large breeds after a meal. It can kill within hours. Go to an emergency vet immediately.

What should I do if my dog eats something toxic?

Call a vet or an animal poison line at once with the product name and the amount. Do not make the dog vomit unless a professional tells you to. Keep the packaging to show them.

My dog got stung by a bee. What now?

Scrape the stinger out, hold a cold compress on the spot and watch the dog for 30 minutes. Swelling of the face, trouble breathing, vomiting or collapse means an emergency vet visit right away.
